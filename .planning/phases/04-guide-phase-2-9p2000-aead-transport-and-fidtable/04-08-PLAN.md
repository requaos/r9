Each is absorbed in its standard 9P2000 form, whole, exactly as `absorb_plain` took the same kind of message in the handshake: `size[4] type[1] tag[2]` then the standard body, with `size` counting those bytes; for `Twrite` the body is `fid[4] offset[8] count[4] data[count]` and for `Rread` `count[4] data[count]`, the data unpadded. Sealed bytes, the 28-byte header, TLVs, page padding and the MAC are not absorbed. `Trekey` and `Rrekey` of every kind and all other traffic sealed during the exchange are not absorbed. The exchange is strictly serial (one request, then its reply), so the wire order is the same at both ends.
- Independent expected bytes: `aead_transport_rekey_wire_keys_and_confirm_equal_test_side_derivation` never calls `Session::open`, `Session::receive`, `open_packet`, `msg::decode_body`, `msg::data_layout`, `verify_rekey_confirm` or any accessor of the session's transcript or keys to obtain an expected value. It works on the bytes recorded off the link with its own `DirectionKeys` from `crypto::session::{Transcript, derive}`, using `keystream`, `mac()` and `rekey_confirm` only; offsets, the page size and the block counters are literals in the test, and the standard 9P2000 frames it absorbs are written with test-side byte helpers. `derive`, `Transcript::chain`, `keystream`, `PacketMac` and `rekey_confirm` are themselves pinned against the Phase 3 primitives in plan 04-03.
