Starting hypothesis for N (planner estimate from research's 13 HVF boots; replaced by the measurement):
- tier-1 sweep, about 130 forks per boot, failed 2 of 3: point estimate of the per-fork rate about 0.0084 (from 1 - p_boot = (1-p)^130 with p_boot = 2/3).
- At that point estimate a smoke boot (about 10 forks) fails about 8% of the time, so 36 green smoke boots give 0.92^36 < 0.05, and 5 green tier-1 sweeps give (1/3)^5 < 0.005. These figures are too small: they assume the true rate equals the observed one.
- The rule that survives re-measurement (D-02) uses the LOWER one-sided 95% confidence bound p_lo of the measured per-fork rate. A series proves a fix only if a still-broken kernel would rarely survive it, and the still-broken kernel hardest to catch is the one with the lowest rate consistent with what was measured. N = ceil(ln(0.05) / (forks_per_boot x ln(1 - p_lo))), about 3/p_lo forks in total. A larger rate gives a smaller N, so the upper bound and the point estimate are never used.
- Worked on research's own sample: 2 failing sweeps of 3 has a lower bound of 0.135 per sweep (the q solving 3q^2 - 2q^3 = 0.05), which is p_lo about 0.0011 per fork at 130 forks. That gives 21 tier-1 sweeps and about 270 smoke boots, against 5 and 36 from the point estimate. This 0.0011 is a planning illustration only. It is never used as a fallback: the new series' own bound replaces it whenever the series has at least one fire, and a series with no fire refutes it (Task 1, rule 5).
